<h2>Speaker &amp; Title</h2>

<p>Emily Blanchard (Dartmouth College), "Justice for Sale: Explaining the Rise in Investor-State Dispute Settlement" (joint work with Monika Sztajerowska, IMF)</p>

<h2>Abstract</h2>

<p>We assemble a novel dataset linking the universe of publicly-known investor–state dispute settlement (ISDS) cases from 2000 to 2020 to litigants’ identities and their global multinational enterprise (MNE) networks. We document a sharp rise in ISDS cases since the mid-2000s, only partly explained by expanded treaty coverage and MNE activity and accompanied by a marked shift in litigant composition toward smaller firms. Around the same time, third-party funding (TPF)— in which external financiers cover litigation costs in exchange for a share of any eventual award—entered international arbitration. We develop a model of firms’ decisions to initiate ISDS litigation and provide the first systematic evidence linking those decisions to global MNE networks and TPF availability. The evidence is consistent with TPF expanding access to litigation, raising claimant win rates, and increasing subsequent filings by other firms, as funder participation signals the feasibility of claims against a given host country. We further show that TPF-backed cases are followed by lower MNE market entry on average, but higher entry by larger firms with treaty-access to ISDS, particularly in host countries with poor investment climates. These results highlight a potential tradeoff for host countries between greater exposure to investor claims and stronger investment incentives, with direct implications for current debates over ISDS reform and regulation of third-party funding.</p>
