<h2>Speaker &amp; Title</h2>

<p>Dan Mattingly (Yale), "AI Slopaganda and Its Limits"</p>

<h2>Abstract</h2>

<p>There is growing concern that AI-generated propaganda, or “slopaganda,” can sway political views. Does the pro-CCP messaging embedded in popular Chinese AI models persuade global audiences that China's political system is superior? We conduct randomized experiments in 12 countries examining how Chinese AI shapes attitudes toward China. In the first study, exposure to DeepSeek, a widely used and consistently pro-CCP model, produces only modest effects on attitudes toward China. These effects do not increase with more intensive use and disappear after a week. In a follow-up experiment, we test additional Chinese AI models and find that they are less persuasive than American AI models and other forms of Chinese propaganda, including Chinese state media and pro-China TikTok creators. In contrast to work showing AI's persuasive power, we find limited effects of authoritarian “slopaganda” abroad, possibly because China's domestic political controls on AI undermine its persuasiveness among international audiences.</p>
