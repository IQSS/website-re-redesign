<h2>From “Is This for Me?” to “Ship Sails”</h2>
<p>Faculty move through the Foundry at different speeds and often do not go through every stage. If this is your first time exploring commercialization possibilities, you might have a lot of questions that need to be addressed first.</p><p>Most of the faculty we work with follow some version of the following path:</p>
<p>You might be here if:</p><ul><li>You are curious but skeptical.</li><li>You are unsure whether any of your work is commercializable.</li><li>Your main questions are about fit, conflicts of interest, and career implications.</li></ul><p>At this stage, we:</p><ul><li>Explain concretely what “commercialization” can mean in your case (and what it does not mean).</li><li>Walk through example paths that similar faculty have taken.</li><li>Identify parts of your work that might plausibly have external users, customers, or partners.</li><li>Give you enough information to decide whether to move forward—or not.</li></ul><p><strong>Outcome:</strong> You come away with clarity. For many, that clarity is “not now.” For others, it is “this might be worth a small experiment.”</p>
<img src="../images/foundry-timeline-stage-0.png" alt="Stage 0: Exploration. &quot;Is this for me?&quot;">
<p>You might be here if:</p><ul><li>You suspect there is something in your research that others would pay for or adopt.</li><li>You do not know what “the product” is, who the user is, or what problem you are really solving.</li><li>You are interested partly because this could also inform what you work on next.</li></ul><p>At this stage, we:</p><ul><li>Help you inventory your existing and planned research through a commercialization lens.</li><li>Identify candidate ideas, tools, datasets, methods, or platforms that might have external demand.</li><li>Conduct lightweight market and stakeholder exploration to see what resonates.</li><li>Use what we learn to refine both the practical opportunity and, if you wish, your research questions.</li></ul><p><strong>Outcome:</strong> You get a sharper sense of whether there is a real opportunity, what it might look like, and whether it is worth treating as “one more project” in your portfolio.</p>
<img src="../images/foundry-timeline-stage-1.png" alt="Stage 1: Finding the Idea. &quot;What here could actually go out into the world?&quot;">
<h2>Stage 2: Getting Ready – “I’m in. Now I need help.”</h2>
<p>Once you decide that you want to create something beyond the paper, there are usually two needs.</p>
<div class="card-grid">
<div class="card">
<img src="../images/foundry-timeline-stage-2a-column-ver.png" alt="Stage 2A: Research Resources">
<h3>Research Needs</h3>
<p>You may realize that, to make the opportunity real, you need additional research:</p><ul><li>New data or experiments</li><li>Further validation of a method or model</li><li>Engineering support to turn a prototype into something demonstrable</li></ul><p>Here, we:</p><ul><li>Help you scope what research work is needed to de-risk the opportunity.</li><li>Connect you to the usual IQSS and Harvard mechanisms—grants, technical support, computing, quantitative consulting, etc.—to get that work done.</li></ul>
</div>
<div class="card">
<img src="../images/foundry-timeline-stage-2b-column-ver.png" alt="Stage 2B: Business-Building Resources">
<h3>Industry Needs</h3>
<p>In parallel, you may be ready on the research side but missing all the “business” components:</p><ul><li>Early customers or partners to talk to</li><li>A simple prototype or demo others can use or react to</li><li>A first-pass understanding of the addressable market</li><li>A coherent narrative and pitch for internal and external audiences</li></ul><p>Here, you need a business driver. At this stage we:</p><ul><li>Help identify and recruit that person (from your team or from our network).</li><li>Work with them to run actual customer and stakeholder conversations, not just talk about them.</li><li>Build first versions of the business model and value proposition.</li><li>Assemble the materials needed to approach funders, customers, or institutional partners.</li></ul>
</div>
</div>
<p>When the startup (or equivalent structure) is ready to emerge, we help you:</p><ul><li>Decide the right vehicle: a venture-backed startup, a customer-funded company, a public-benefit structure, a license to an existing firm, or a partnership with a public or nonprofit organization.</li><li>Identify and approach the right early funders or first customers.</li><li>Choose and form the appropriate legal entity (LLC, C‑corp, etc.).</li><li>Navigate Harvard’s conflict-of-interest policies and technology transfer processes.</li><li>Lead or support negotiations between Harvard and the new entity to align incentives and responsibilities.</li><li>Line up essential services—legal, accounting, HR, data infrastructure, compute, and more.</li></ul><p><strong>Outcome:</strong> A deal is made. There is now an actual company or organizational vehicle, with defined relationships among Harvard, you, and the new entity.</p>
<img src="../images/foundry-timeline-stage-3.png" alt="Stage 3: The First Deal. &quot;Turning this into an actual entity.&quot;">
<p>Once the company is formed and the initial deal is in place, there is often a defined period—typically on the order of 12–18 months—during which the new entity can:</p><ul><li>Operate on Harvard property</li><li>Make use of certain Harvard resources under agreed terms</li><li>Continue to interact with your lab and research program in defined ways</li></ul><p>During this period, we:</p><ul><li>Help ensure that everyone understands and abides by the boundaries set in the agreements.</li><li>Support the gradual operational and organizational separation between the company and the university.</li><li>Work with you and the business driver to prepare for life fully outside Harvard.</li></ul><p><strong>Outcome:</strong> The company grows while still benefiting from a structured, time-limited connection to your academic environment.</p>
<img src="../images/foundry-timeline-stage-4.png" alt="Stage 4: Runway. &quot;On Harvard's campus, with a clock.&quot;">
<p>At some point, the clock runs out by design. The company:</p><ul><li>Moves fully off campus</li><li>Operates on its own infrastructure, with its own staff and governance</li><li>Interacts with you and Harvard at arm’s length, like any other external partner</li></ul><p>Our job at this point is mostly complete. We remain available as a connector and resource, but the company is independent. You remain a faculty member, with whatever formal relationship to the company was established (e.g., advisor, board member, founder equity), and with a new channel for your work to reach the world.</p><p><strong>Outcome:</strong> The ship sails. You stay with it as an advisor, keeping an ownership stake and continuing to provide your input, but on a part-time or consulting basis. You stay on the faculty.</p>
