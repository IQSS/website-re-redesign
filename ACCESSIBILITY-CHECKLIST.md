# Accessibility checklist: IQSS Hugo rebuild

**Draft for Liz's review.** Every check below comes from Harvard Digital Accessibility Services (DAS) guidance at <https://accessibility.huit.harvard.edu/>. Nothing has been added from general WCAG knowledge. Points Harvard's guidance does not cover are listed separately at the end, under "Not in Harvard's guidance". The same items, with blank "Liz decision" and "Notes" columns, are in `ACCESSIBILITY-CHECKLIST.csv`.

## The standard Harvard requires

> "the Digital Accessibility Standards (or "the Standards") are The World Wide Web Consortium's Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version 2.1, Level AA Conformance (WCAG 2.1 Level AA) for websites and web-based applications and services."
> — [Harvard University Digital Accessibility Policy](https://accessibility.huit.harvard.edu/digital-accessibility-policy) (last updated June 1, 2023)

Dates and requirements from the Policy and its [Implementation Procedures](https://accessibility.huit.harvard.edu/digital-accessibility-policy-procedures):

- **June 1, 2023.** From this date, University IT "newly purchased, licensed, or internally developed" is "expected to conform to the Standards to the fullest extent possible at the point of rollout or implementation". New University Content posted from this date "should aim to" conform "to the extent technically feasible". My reading is that a rebuilt site counts as "internally developed" and so falls under the first rule. That reading is mine, not Harvard's, so Liz should confirm it.
- **Existing sites and content.** The Accessibility Steering Committee (ASC) sets priorities for improving them. It may require a remediation plan, including for "a site created or revised prior to December 1, 2019".
- **Every page** should link to the Harvard Digital Accessibility Policy. The Procedures suggest doing this in the footer.
- **Video and audio.** "All videos posted to University Websites and platforms must include synchronized, accurate, closed captions and should aim to include descriptive audio where applicable." Audio such as podcasts needs accurate transcripts.
- **Exceptions.** An exception needs a Policy Exception Request Form sent to the ASC, along with an Equally Effective Alternate Access Plan (EEAAP). The ASC reviews requests within 30 days.
- **Testing.** The Procedures call for automated scanning first, then manual testing (assistive technology, keyboard-only testing and content review), and an expert review as needed.

## How to read this checklist

- **WCAG**: the success-criterion number(s) that the cited Harvard page itself references. The column is blank where the page gives none.
- **Level**: Harvard's pages do not give a conformance level for individual criteria; they only state that the Policy requires WCAG 2.1 AA overall. The column is therefore blank throughout. Liz may fill it in (see the "Not in Harvard's guidance" section).
- **How to check** uses four methods: automated scan, template review (checking the Hugo layouts and partials once), manual keyboard or screen-reader test, and content review by a person (page by page).
- Several DAS pages are word-for-word duplicates of each other. These pairs were merged: keyboard = provide-logical-and-visible-focus-indication; custom-widgets-and-controls = provide-name-role-and-value-information; notifications-and-alerts = provide-helpful-error-messages; structure-developers = provide-accessible-labels-and-instructions. Two more paths redirect to pages already covered: /avoid-reliance-color goes to presentation-developers, and /support-keyboard-interaction goes to keyboard.

## Not in Harvard's guidance: for Liz to decide

> **These points do NOT come from Harvard's DAS pages.** They come from general knowledge of WCAG and are listed here only for Liz to decide whether to add them. They are not part of the numbered checklist above or the CSV.

**Moved into the checklist after the remaining DAS pages were read:**

- **Moving or auto-updating content (WCAG 2.2.2)** is now **MED-13**. Source: [Design for comprehension](https://accessibility.huit.harvard.edu/design-comprehension), which asks whether users have "a way … to pause or stop the moving content".
- **Flashing (WCAG 2.3.1)** is now **MED-14**. Same source. Harvard states its test as a flash rate "outside of the danger rate of between 2 and 55 Hz", or a warning on links to the page. See point 5 below for how that differs from WCAG's wording.

**Still not covered by Harvard's guidance:**

1. **Criteria Harvard cites that fall outside WCAG 2.1 AA.** Harvard's pages refer to 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) and 3.2.6 Consistent Help, which are WCAG 2.2 criteria. They also refer to 2.5.5 Target Size (Enhanced), which is Level AAA, and to 4.1.1 Parsing, which WCAG 2.2 makes obsolete. The Policy itself requires 2.1 AA. Should FRM-14 (target size) and HDG-11 (validation) be treated as required or as good practice?
2. **Per-criterion levels.** Harvard does not state an A/AA level for each criterion. Should the Level column be filled in from WCAG itself?
3. **Page language (WCAG 3.1.1).** Harvard covers marking *changes* of language (3.1.2, item PL-05) but not declaring the page's main language (`<html lang="en">`).
4. **No unexpected change of context (WCAG 3.2.1 On Focus, 3.2.2 On Input).** This applies to listing filters that reload or re-sort results as soon as an option is chosen. Harvard's guidance does not cover it.
5. **How the flashing test is worded (MED-14).** Harvard words it as a 2-55 Hz danger range. WCAG 2.3.1's own test is nothing flashing more than three times in any one-second period, unless the flashes are below the general and red flash thresholds. Which wording should the checklist use?
6. **Links that open a new tab or window.** Harvard gives no guidance on warning users. This is relevant to share buttons and external links.
7. **Inline SVG icons.** Harvard covers images, icon fonts and CSS background images (IMG-09/10/15), but not inline SVG, which Hugo templates often use for icons (e.g. `role="img"` with a title, or `aria-hidden`).
8. **Focus hidden by sticky elements (WCAG 2.2: 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured).** Harvard warns that sticky elements can obscure *content* (TYP-08), but does not say anything about the keyboard focus indicator.
9. **Calendar widget specifics.** Harvard has no guidance written for calendars, only the general custom-widget items (DYN-01 to DYN-10) and a pointer to the ARIA Authoring Practices patterns. Liz may want a calendar-specific test script, e.g. moving between dates, announcing the month, and events on each day.
10. **Third-party embeds.** Harvard's Procedures count "calendar tools, maps, social media embeds, and media players" as University IT covered by the Policy, but give no checks specific to embeds beyond the media-player items.
11. **Reduced-motion preference (`prefers-reduced-motion`).** Harvard asks for pause/stop controls (MED-13) but does not mention the CSS preference.
12. **Video-only content (no audio track).** Harvard covers captions, transcripts and audio description for video with sound, and transcripts for audio-only, but not a text alternative for silent video.

## Conflicting guidance within Harvard's pages

- **Screen readers.** DAS's screen-reader guides recommend Chrome for JAWS and NVDA. The developer "Solid Structure" page suggests JAWS with Edge or Firefox, and NVDA with Firefox.
- **Form errors.** "Provide helpful error messages" says to move focus to the list of errors. The "Form error communication" technique announces errors through a live region without moving focus. FRM-09 accepts either.
